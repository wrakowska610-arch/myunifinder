#My Uni Finder

Website: my-uni-finder.com 

A landing page that helps students find and compare universities abroad by tuition fees, study programmes, language requirements and scholarships.

#Business Idea

*Business name* My Uni Finder
*Service* Helping students find, compare and fund studies at universities abroad
*Target audience* Students who want to study in another country
*What makes it different* Search, cost comparison and scholarships all on one simple page: no jumping between dozens of university websites
*Main call to action* "Find your university": search universities by country and field of study 

#Logo

The name *My Uni Finder* says what the site does: it helps students find and compare universities abroad. The logo is saved as `logo.svg`.

Font: Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif. It is clean, easy to read and works on every device.

#User Stories

#User story 1: Find universities (main story)

As a *student*, I want to *search for universities abroad by country*, so that *I can find study opportunities that interest me*.

*Acceptance criteria*
- The website provides a list of countries to choose from.
- Selecting a country displays universities located there.
- Each university listing shows its name and country.

*Implementation* the *Find your university* section (`#universities`) has a country dropdown (20 countries), a field-of-study filter, a search button and a reset button. Results show each university's name, city and country. The hero "Find your university" button and the final call to action both link to this section.

#User story 2: Compare study costs

As a *prospective international student*, I want to *compare tuition fees at different universities*, so that *I can choose an option that fits my budget*.

*Acceptance criteria*
- Each university profile displays available tuition fee information.
- Tuition fees include the currency and relevant study period.
- Users can view tuition fees for at least two universities side by side.

*Implementation* the *Compare Costs* section (`#cost-comparison`). Users save universities as favourites and compare their costs, locations and study languages side by side.

#User story 3: Find scholarships

As a *student planning to study abroad*, I want to *find scholarship opportunities*, so that *I can reduce the cost of my education*.

*Acceptance criteria*
- The website has a section listing scholarship opportunities.
- Each scholarship listing includes eligibility requirements and a deadline when available.
- Each listing links to the scholarship provider's information or application page.

*Implementation* the *Scholarship Finder* section (`#scholarship-finder`) shows scholarship cards for the countries of the user's saved universities. Each card has a description, eligibility details and a link to the official application website.

#Page Structure

1. *Header and navigation* logo, plus links to Universities, Compare Costs, Scholarships and About Us
2. *Hero*: headline, short description and the main call-to-action button
3. *Features / Why choose us* (`#about`): three benefits: Discover Universities, Compare Study Costs, Find Scholarships
4. *University search* (`#universities`)
5. *Cost comparison* (`#cost-comparison`)
6. *Scholarship finder* (`#scholarship-finder`)
7. *Final call to action* "There is a university out there for you."
8. *Footer*: navigation links, contact email (`hello@myunifinder.example`), back-to-top link
9. *Cookie notice* accept / reject buttons

The page uses semantic HTML: `<header>`, `<nav>`, `<main>`, `<section>`, `<article>`, `<footer>`.
